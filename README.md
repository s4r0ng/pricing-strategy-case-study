# pricing-strategy-case-study
Nike Air Max: Pricing Strategy Case Study

Why does a shoe cost ₹12,000 when making it costs ₹800?

A self-built learning case study that applies core pricing concepts to Nike Air Max in the Indian market. It looks at how price works as a brand signal, not just a number on a tag.

#Overview

An Air Max costs roughly ₹800 to make, yet customers happily pay ₹12,000 to ₹20,000 and sometimes queue for it. The gap isn't foam and stitching. It is identity, aspiration, and belonging. This case study explains how Nike builds and protects that gap.

#Key Numbers (approximate)
Manufacturing cost	~₹800 per pair
Air Max MRP (India)	 ₹10,000+
Price premium ~15x over cost

#What the Case Study Covers
Four pricing approaches: cost-plus, competitive, value-based, and psychological, and why Nike relies on value-based pricing
Price as a positioning statement: how the same shoe at ₹2,500, ₹6,000, ₹12,000+ or ₹25,000+ tells a different story
Psychological pricing: charm pricing, anchoring, the decoy effect, and bundle pricing
Price elasticity: why strong brand identity makes Nike's demand inelastic and gives it pricing power
3-SKU architecture: how Air Max SC Running (₹7,000), Air Max 90 (₹12,000) and Air Max 97 (₹13,500) steer buyers toward the target product
Five pricing lessons from Nike

#Key Takeaways
Nike prices on perceived value, not manufacturing cost.
Price signals identity before the customer touches the product.
A 3-SKU line-up uses the decoy effect to guide buyers.
Strong brand identity creates inelastic demand.
Never discount the flagship, because it trains customers to wait and weakens the brand signal.

#The biggest lesson: pricing too low is often riskier than pricing too high. A low price doesn't say "accessible". It says "we don't fully believe in what we made."
